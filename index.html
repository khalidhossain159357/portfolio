PROJECT 01

Global Layoffs and Workforce Reduction Analysis

Google Sheets, BigQuery, SQL, Google Data Studio

928.66K layoffs across 4,537 events analyzed
Read case study

Problem

When did layoffs happen, where were they concentrated, and which companies returned to layoffs again and again?

What I did

Audited 4,562 raw records in Google Sheets, then cleaned and deduplicated them to 4,560 in BigQuery. I engineered date and funding features, used LAG() to measure the gap between layoff rounds, and built reusable SQL views behind a three-page dashboard.

Findings

  • Layoffs spiked in late 2022 and early 2023, covering 2,954 companies, with an average reduction of 29.48%.
  • Amazon (59.3K), Intel (43.1K) and Meta (35.7K) reported the most layoffs. Retail led industries at 108.2K.
  • 71.8% of company groupings had one layoff, 18.1% had repeats and 10.1% had multiple rounds. Amazon had 21 rounds and Google 19.
  • Post-IPO companies accounted for about 583.95K layoffs, and the $1B+ funding range for about 277.7K.
  • The United States dominated the geography.

Funding results are descriptive, not causal.

PROJECT 02

Real Estate Sales and Customer Analysis

Excel, Power Query, MySQL, SQL, Power BI

$52.54M revenue, 73.03% sell-through
Read case study

Problem

Where does revenue come from, which buildings and customers perform best, and what should the business do next?

What I did

Cleaned the data with Excel and Power Query, built a relational MySQL database with a foreign-key link between customers and properties, answered twelve business questions in SQL, and created eight reporting views for a two-page Power BI dashboard.

Findings

  • 195 of 267 properties sold. Revenue peaked in 2007 at about $27.45M, driven by volume rather than price.
  • Building 2 sold 94.74% of its properties. Building 5 sold only 36.54%.
  • Website acquisition generated about $32.65M. Home buyers brought $33.36M against $19.18M from investors, who bought more repeatedly.
  • Customers aged 40 to 49 generated the most revenue. California contributed about $33M.

I recommended investigating Building 5, building a repeat-buyer strategy, and reducing dependence on California.

PROJECT 03

Tracking Student Engagement

MySQL, Python, Excel

Engagement vs. certificates: r = 0.51
Read case study

Problem

How does engagement differ between free and paying students, did it change from Q2 2021 to Q2 2022, and does watching more lead to more certificates?

What I did

Prepared subscription and engagement data in SQL, removed outliers in Python, ran confidence intervals, F-tests and t-tests in Excel, and built a linear regression for certificates.

Findings

  • Free-plan average watch time rose from 14.21 to 16.04 minutes. Paying students fell from 360.10 to 292.22 but stayed far above free users.
  • Watch time and certificates had a moderate positive correlation (r = 0.51).
  • Only 640 students were active in both periods, so engagement across the two quarters was not independent.
  • Watch time alone explained about 30.5% of certificate variation (R² = 0.305).

Dataset and scenario are from the 365 Data Science project. The SQL, analysis and write-up are my own.

PROJECT 04

Customer Segmentation for Marketing

Python, K-Means, Scikit-learn, Pandas

3,725 customers in 4 segments
Read case study

Problem

Which customer groups exist based on engagement and lifetime value, and which deserve retention, upselling or re-engagement?

What I did

Removed records with missing watch time, applied a log transform to skewed engagement, standardized features and chose four clusters using silhouette, Davies-Bouldin and Calinski-Harabasz scores. I then profiled segments by region and acquisition channel.

Findings

  • Premium Power Users were only 5.4% of customers but had the highest value (average CLV 309.48).
  • High-Value Engaged customers were the largest valuable group at 37.5%.
  • Engaged Low-Value customers (33.8%, CLV 54.90) are a monetization opportunity.
  • Moderate-Value Inactive customers (23.3%) watched a median of 49 minutes, a re-engagement opportunity.

Dataset and scenario are from the 365 Data Science project. The preprocessing, modeling and analysis are my own.

PROJECT 05

Bike Sales Dashboard

Microsoft Excel, PivotTables, PivotCharts

481 of 1,000 customers bought a bike
Read case study

Problem

Which customer traits are associated with buying a bike?

What I did

Cleaned 1,026 records down to 1,000 unique customers by removing 26 duplicates and standardizing categories, created age brackets, then built PivotTables, PivotCharts and an interactive Excel dashboard.

Findings

  • Overall purchase rate was 48.1%. Singles bought at 54.1% against 42.9% for married customers.
  • Customers commuting 2 to 5 miles bought at 58.6%. Those commuting 10 or more miles bought at 29.7%.
  • Customers with no car bought at 61.1%, against 35.6% for those with four cars.
  • The Pacific region led at 58.9%.

These are associations, not causes.
